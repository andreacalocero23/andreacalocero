Project Context

This project was developed during the Marine Policy course as part of my MSc in Marine Biology. The assignment consisted of designing a hypothetical Marine Protected Area anywhere in the world while respecting realistic ecological, legal, political and financial constraints. I selected the coast of Maratea (Basilicata, Southern Italy), one of the most biodiverse stretches of coastline in the region.

The ecological background, policy framework and conservation information were derived from scientific publications, official legislation and institutional reports. However, the proposed Marine Protected Area — including site selection, conservation strategy, stakeholder engagement, management actions, implementation priorities, partnership network and budget allocation — was independently designed and developed as a hypothetical conservation proposal.

The objective was to produce a complete conservation proposal integrating marine ecology, environmental policy, protected area management, stakeholder engagement and financial planning into a realistic framework.

Why Maratea?

The coast of Maratea overlooks the Tyrrhenian Sea and is characterised by high biodiversity due to its particular habitats, including sea caves, cliffs, rocky reefs and Posidonia oceanica meadows. This stretch of coastline hosts numerous species of conservation concern and represents one of the most ecologically valuable areas in the region.

Several factors make Maratea particularly suitable for enhanced marine protection:

High Biodiversity

Rich marine communities including Posidonia oceanica meadows, rocky reefs and sea caves

Protected Species

Presence of Caretta caretta, Cladocora caespitosa and other species of conservation concern

Tourism & Fisheries

Seaside tourism and traditional fisheries are key economic activities

Landscape Value

Outstanding natural beauty, cultural heritage and archaeological significance

Conservation Objectives

Protect Marine Biodiversity

Safeguard the rich marine communities and species of conservation concern along the Maratea coast.

Preserve Critical Habitats

Protect Posidonia oceanica meadows, rocky reefs, sea caves and other essential habitats.

Improve Ecosystem Resilience

Enhance the resilience of marine ecosystems to climate change and anthropogenic pressures.

Promote Sustainable Tourism

Support eco-tourism and sustainable recreational activities that benefit local communities.

Support Local Fisheries

Improve fish stocks through spillover effects and sustainable fisheries management.

Enhance Environmental Education

Promote awareness and understanding of marine conservation among local communities.

Ecological Framework

The Maratea coast hosts a remarkable diversity of marine habitats, including:

Posidonia oceanica Meadows

Essential habitat providing oxygen, biodiversity and coastal protection

Rocky Reefs

Complex substrates supporting diverse benthic communities

Sea Caves

Unique habitats hosting specialised species communities

Coastal Cliffs

Important nesting and resting areas for marine species

The area hosts species of conservation concern including Caretta caretta (loggerhead turtle), Cladocora caespitosa, yellow gorgonians, sea urchins, octopus, sea stars and numerous commercially important fish species. These habitats provide essential ecosystem services including nursery grounds, carbon sequestration, coastal protection and biodiversity support.

Policy and Legal Framework

The proposal was developed within the context of existing environmental legislation and conservation policies:

Italian Legislation

National protected area laws and marine conservation regulations

European Directives

Marine Strategy Framework Directive · Habitats Directive

Natura 2000

Three existing Natura 2000 sites: IT9210160, IT9210155, IT9210015

Existing Regulations

Ordinance 9/93 protecting the Santo Janni islet archaeological site

These frameworks influenced the proposed management strategy, providing the legal basis for enhanced marine protection and ensuring alignment with established conservation principles.

MPA Design

The proposed "Tyrrhenian Pearl MPA" integrates the existing Natura 2000 sites already present in the area — primarily focused on terrestrial biodiversity conservation — by merging their marine portions into one large, cohesive Marine Protected Area that can effectively protect the marine ecosystems of the Maratea coast.

The MPA design includes:

Protection Objectives

Clear conservation goals for biodiversity and habitat protection

Management Priorities

Identification of key actions and implementation strategies

Zoning Concepts

Spatial planning to balance conservation and human activities

Monitoring & Adaptive Management

Long-term monitoring to evaluate effectiveness and adapt management

The proposal includes implementation priorities and a long-term conservation vision, ensuring the MPA can achieve its ecological and socio-economic objectives.

The budget allocation reflects the priorities of the MPA proposal, with a focus on communication, education and stakeholder engagement. Additional funding could be sought from regional, national and European sources.

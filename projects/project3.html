Project Overview

This field monitoring project investigated the impact of river runoff on marine ecosystems along the Civitanova coast (Central Adriatic Sea). The study focused on the Chienti River plume and its interaction with the Western Adriatic Coastal Current (WACC), evaluating how water currents influence the spatial distribution of organic matter, nutrients, and biological communities.

The research was conducted as part of the Marine Monitoring course at UNIVPM, with field sampling carried out aboard the R/V Anna Guidotti. The project integrated physical-chemical measurements, biological indicators, and statistical analyses to assess environmental quality and ecological status according to the Marine Strategy Framework Directive (MSFD 2008/56/EC).

Scientific Background

Rivers are fundamental for human civilization but represent major sources of natural and anthropogenic pollution to coastal marine ecosystems. The Chienti River, a 91 km watercourse of the Central Apennines, discharges into the Adriatic Sea near Civitanova Marche, transporting inorganic nutrients, organic matter, and contaminants from agricultural and urban runoff.

The Western Adriatic Coastal Current (WACC) flows predominantly southward, transporting sediments and nutrients from riverine sources. Bora winds strengthen this southward flow, while Scirocco winds can temporarily slow it down, creating asymmetrical impacts on environmental quality and biological communities.

The presence of river runoff can influence the achievement of Good Environmental Status according to the MSFD (2008/56/EC), particularly affecting Descriptors 5 (Eutrophication), 8 (Contaminants), 9 (Contaminants in seafood), and 10 (Marine Litter).

Methodology

Field Sampling

Sampling was conducted aboard the R/V Anna Guidotti (IMO: 9096686, LOA: 30.99 m, Beam: 6 m). Three transects (T1, T3, T4) were sampled at increasing distances from the Chienti River mouth to evaluate the spatial gradient of riverine influence.

  • Transect 1 — closest to river mouth (highest influence)
  • Transect 3 — intermediate distance
  • Transect 4 — farthest downstream (lowest influence)

Physical-Chemical Parameters

Physical-chemical parameters were measured in water and sediment samples to characterise environmental conditions and detect riverine influence.

  • CTD Profiling — Temperature, Salinity, Depth along the water column
  • Total Suspended Matter (TSM) — water column
  • Inorganic Matter (IM) — water column analysis
  • Total Organic Matter (TOM) — sediment analysis
  • Organic Matter (OM) — sediment analysis

Biological Indicators: Water Column

Biological indicators in the water column were analysed to assess phytoplankton biomass and zooplankton community structure.

  • Phytopigments (Chlorophyll-a) — indicator of phytoplankton biomass
  • Zooplankton Analysis — assessment of abundance, Shannon Index, and Pielou's Evenness

Biological Indicators: Benthic Fauna

Benthic communities were analysed to detect organic enrichment and ecological stress using established ecological indices.

  • Meiofauna — quantification of small benthic organisms (nematodes, copepods)
  • Nematodes / Copepods Ratio (N/C) — indicator of organic enrichment
  • Macrofauna — abundance, taxa richness, diversity indices
  • Shannon-Wiener Index (H') — diversity assessment
  • Pielou's Evenness Index (J') — community evenness

Results

The results revealed significant patterns in organic load distribution, benthic community structure, and zooplankton response along the river-coast gradient.

Discussion

The results provide evidence supporting the hypothesis (H₁) that river runoff significantly affects environmental quality and biological communities along the Civitanova coast, with asymmetrical impacts driven by coastal currents.

The N/C ratio exceeding 20 in all transects confirms organic enrichment throughout the study area, but the highest values in Transect 4 (downstream) suggest that the Western Adriatic Coastal Current transports organic matter southward, creating a gradient of impact.

The macrofauna and meiofauna community structures reflect this gradient, with lower diversity indices and altered taxa richness in the downstream transect. This pattern is consistent with the hypothesis of current-driven organic load leading to ecological disequilibrium.

The zooplankton response adds another dimension, with higher diversity in nutrient-enriched areas suggesting a bottom-up effect on the pelagic food web. These findings highlight the integrated nature of riverine impacts on coastal marine ecosystems and the importance of current dynamics in shaping ecological patterns.

Conclusions

The Marine Monitoring Project has demonstrated that river runoff significantly impacts marine ecosystems along the Civitanova coast, with asymmetrical effects driven by the Western Adriatic Coastal Current. The combination of physical-chemical parameters, benthic community indices, and planktonic indicators provided a comprehensive assessment of environmental quality.

The study highlights the importance of integrated monitoring approaches for assessing Good Environmental Status under the MSFD, and contributes valuable baseline data for future studies on the impacts of riverine inputs on coastal biodiversity and ecosystem functioning.

This project reinforced my understanding of marine monitoring methodologies, ecological indicator analysis, and the complex interactions between physical oceanography and biological communities in coastal ecosystems.

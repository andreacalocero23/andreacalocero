Project Context

This project was developed during the Master's Degree course in Marine Protected Areas. Each student group was assigned an existing Marine Protected Area and asked to conduct an extensive scientific and management review. Our group was assigned the Cerbère-Banyuls Marine Nature Reserve, one of the oldest and most renowned Marine Protected Areas in the Mediterranean Sea. The project concluded with a scientific presentation delivered to the rest of the class and to Professor Carlo Cerrano, marine zoologist and expert in Marine Protected Areas.

The objective was to investigate the reserve through an in-depth analysis of its history, governance, biodiversity, habitats, management strategies, stakeholder engagement, scientific monitoring, educational activities and conservation outcomes. Most of the information was obtained from scientific publications, official reports and institutional documents.

However, unlike the descriptive sections, the final proposal for expanding the Marine Protected Area boundaries and introducing new enhanced protection zones was independently developed by our student group as part of the project.

History of the Marine Reserve

1965

Local Initiative Begins

The municipality initiated the project following the request of local residents concerned about the protection of marine resources.

1971

Scientific Support

The Arago Laboratory presented a scientific report in support of the creation of the reserve, providing the necessary scientific evidence.

1974

Official Establishment

Creation of the Cerbère-Banyuls Marine Nature Reserve, managed by the local people through a collaborative governance model.

1977

Regional Management

The regional government of Pyrénées-Orientales took over the management of the reserve, strengthening its institutional framework.

1990

Strengthened Management

The decree 90-790 replaced the initial one, involving stakeholders more closely and reinforcing the protection level of the area.

2018

International Recognition

Awarded IUCN Green List status and recognised as a global marine refugee site by the Marine Conservation Institute in Seattle.

2019

SPAMI Designation

Entered the list of Specially Protected Areas of Mediterranean Importance (SPAMI), recognising its regional conservation value.

2022

Expansion Process Initiated

A project to extend the perimeter of the reserve started, with extensive stakeholder consultation and public participation.

Management and Governance

The Cerbère-Banyuls Marine Reserve is considered an excellent example of collaborative governance. It is part of the MEDPAN network and its board of directors is made of 35 members representing a wide range of stakeholders.

11 Public Administrators

7 Local Elected Officials

6 Scientists

Industrial & Recreational Fishing Reps

6 Aquatic Sports Representatives

3 Other Stakeholder Representatives

The reserve maintains a Scientific Council with 16 members, established in 2000, to guide research and monitoring activities. Technical staff, law enforcement cooperation and stakeholder involvement ensure effective management and conservation outcomes.

Biodiversity and Habitats

The Cerbère-Banyuls Marine Reserve hosts an extraordinary diversity of marine life, with 1,239 animal species and 497 plant species recorded within its boundaries. Among these, 49 species are protected under national, European or international legislation.

The reserve's key habitats include:

Coralligenous Reefs

The most important habitat-type, forming extensions of rocky outcrops along the 6 km coast

Posidonia oceanica Meadows

Essential seagrass habitats supporting high biodiversity and ecosystem services

Rocky Reefs

Complex substrates supporting diverse benthic communities

Caves and Cracks

Hosting many different types of life, including specialised cave-dwelling species

The coralligenous reefs are particularly significant, forming extensions of the many rocky outcrops along the coastline. Caves and cracks exhibit many different types of life, contributing to the reserve's exceptional biodiversity.

Scientific Research

The reserve hosts continuous ecological research, with at least a dozen monitoring expeditions carried out each year, either internally, externally or in collaboration with research laboratories.

Proposed Expansion of the Marine Protected Area

This section represents the original contribution of our student group to the project. While the scientific review of the existing reserve was based on published literature and official documents, the following expansion proposal was independently developed by our team as a conservation planning exercise.

Our Group's Proposal

We developed a proposal to extend the reserve boundaries to the south and north, and to establish two new zones of enhanced protection within the expanded perimeter. The proposal considered ecological connectivity, habitat representation and improved conservation effectiveness.

The expansion proposal was developed through stakeholder consultation, including 5 workshops with representatives of sea users, 4 public meetings in Port Vente, Banyuls, Collioure and Cerbère, and a collaborative platform (CartoDébat) to gather community input and ensure participatory planning.
